  2. TrustNLP 25
    “Sorry, Come Again?” Prompting - Enhancing Comprehension and Diminishing Hallucination with [PAUSE]-Injected Optimal Paraphrasing
    Vipula Rawte, S. M. T. Tonmoy, S. M. Zaman, and 4 more authors
    2024
    Abs

    Hallucination remains a critical weakness of large language models (LLMs). We propose the “Sorry, Come Again” (SCA) prompting framework, which combines optimal paraphrasing and strategic insertion of [PAUSE] tokens to slow down generation and promote deeper processing. We analyze linguistic traits such as readability, formality, and concreteness across prompts on 21 LLMs, and show that low clarity leads to more hallucinations. We then identify optimal paraphrases using integrated gradients, and fine-tune models with [PAUSE]-injected inputs. This dual approach notably improves coherence and factuality in generated outputs.

  1. ACL 25
    SEPSIS: I Can Catch Your Lies – A New Paradigm for Deception Detection
    Anku Rani, Dwip Dalal, Shreya Gautam, and 5 more authors
    2023
    Abs

    Deception is the intentional practice of twisting information. This research explores three forms: lies of omission, lies of commission, and lies of influence, focusing primarily on lies of omission. We introduce a novel NLP framework supported by an annotated dataset of 876,784 samples from merged fake-news sources and Times of India headlines. Each sample is labeled for omission type (e.g., speculation, bias), “color” of lie (black/white/gray/red), intent (influence, prestige), and topic (political, educational, religious, etc.). Our multi-task learning pipeline, which merges fine-tuned language models, attains an F1 score of 0.87 across all annotation layers. We also examine correlations between omission-based lies and propaganda techniques—e.g., loaded language often signals opinion—and will release both model and dataset under an open license.

  4. arxiv 23
    RESTORE: Graph Embedding Assessment Through Reconstruction
    Hiu Ying Yip, Chandra Ravuru, Neeloy Banerjee, and 4 more authors
    2023
    Abs

    Following the success of word embeddings, graph embeddings (GEs) have grown in popularity—but intrinsic evaluation methods are lacking. We propose RESTORE, an intrinsic evaluation framework that assesses graph embeddings via their ability to reconstruct the original graph’s topology and semantics. We evaluate on multiple embedding families (factorization-, random-walk-, and deep learning-based) using the CommonSense Knowledge Graph.
